## Rules that apply every day

**The 5 exam rules.** Write these on a card above your desk.
1. First parts first: do part (a) of every Section B question before anything else.
2. If you're stuck for 2 minutes, move on.
3. Use "show that" results in later parts, even if you couldn't prove them.
4. Keep answers to keywords, and inside the box (about 8 words).
5. Sanity-check every number. $9.6M from $100k is wrong, and so is 36,000 m/s for a gas molecule.

## What the terms mean

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **★** | A protected task. If the day shrinks, do the ★ tasks in order and drop the rest. |
| **Full timed paper** | A complete past paper sat under exam conditions: the real time limit, no notes, no mark scheme, phone away. Calculator and data booklet only where the real exam allows them. |
| **Mark it and log it** | Mark your paper against the official mark scheme. Be strict and give only the marks the scheme gives. For every mark you lost, write one line in the mistake log. |
| **Mistake log** | One sheet you keep for all six weeks, with the columns `Date \| Paper + Q \| What I did \| Rule/fix \| Redo date \| Redo ✓`. It's the list of exactly what to fix. |
| **Redo** | Re-attempting a logged question cold, 2–3 days later, without looking at the answer. A line only gets its ✓ once the redo is right. |
| **Box discipline** | Chem (and Physics) written answers: 8 keywords or fewer, inside the answer box. Anything outside the box can be "not marked"; that cost you about 5 Chem marks. |
| **Box-discipline drill** | Take old answers and rewrite them in 8 keywords or fewer, inside a drawn box, using the mark scheme's wording. |
| **Recall cards / flashcards** | Question on the front, one-line answer on the back (e.g. "I₂(aq) colour?" → "brown"). Test yourself and don't just reread. Keep the cards you get wrong. |
| **Kognity** | Your online Chem textbook. Use it for content: read a section, then do its questions. |
| **First parts first / part (a)s** | In Section B (long questions), do part (a) of every question before going deeper. Part (a)s are usually the easiest marks, and you left whole questions blank in the mocks. |
| **Section A / Section B** | Maths papers: A is short questions, B is long multi-part questions (about 50% of the marks). |
| **"Show that"** | A question that gives you the answer. Even if you can't prove it, use the given result in the later parts. |
| **GDC** | Your graphics calculator. Its key functions: numerical integration, solver, invNorm (normal distribution) and intersect. |
| **Sprint** | A short timed drill (for example, 6 part (a)s at 1 minute per mark) to train pace. |
| **Gate check** | Sat 3 Oct: each target topic is green (you can redo the mock question and get 2 fresh ones right) or amber (needs another fix block). |
| **Evidence bank** | For History, 5 specific facts (dates, names, numbers) per likely essay question, ready to use as evidence. |
| **Essay plan (15 min)** | An intro with your judgement and criteria, then 3 claims, each with 2–3 pieces of evidence and a counter-argument. |
| **Criteria A–D** | The English marking criteria. Your teacher's marks per criterion tell you which one to fix. |
| **Listening protocol** | A 20-min exam text (play, answer, replay, check the transcript), then a 10-min active podcast (write down 3 numbers/names and 3 new words). |
| **Weekly review** | 15 min on Sunday: scores, open log lines, which subject is lagging. Next week's time shifts towards it. |
| **P1 / P2 / P3** | Paper 1, 2 and 3 of a subject. For example, Physics P1 is 1A (multiple choice) plus 1B (data/practical). |
